Youth career night: the 10-minute software engineering session

Run three times, once per rotation. The kids in front of you chose this table, so skip the “why should you care” and get to the good stuff. Ages run 11 to 18; the older ones will lean in on pay and college, the younger ones on the song and the code editor, so keep both moving. The page at the QR code is their take-home; everything below is what you do live.

Setup on the table

  • Your phone or laptop open to the page, sound on. A small Bluetooth speaker helps if you have one.
  • The QR poster (qr-poster.html, printed) propped where they can scan it while they sit down.
  • Optional: a printed copy of the pay ladder for anyone without a phone.

The 10 minutes

0:00 to 1:15. Hook. Say one sentence: “Scan the code, then watch this.” Play the one-minute anthem from your device with the volume up. Let them scan and play it on their own phones if they want; a few will.

1:15 to 3:45. Your story. Three beats, no more:

  1. Bachelor’s and master’s in computer science, and an internship while in school that led to your first job. Tell them the internship is the part they should remember.
  2. One of three original engineers on FamilySearch Memories. Millions of families’ photos and recordings live there now, and it was mentioned at General Conference. Have the Memories page open in case someone wants to see it.
  3. Today you lead a team of engineers. Say what a manager actually does in one sentence and that you still get to build.

3:45 to 5:30. What the job feels like. Scroll to the day-in-the-life bars. Say the surprise: only about a third of the week is writing new code. The rest is people, design, and detective work. Then the pay ladder. Say the two striped bars are your own numbers: 65 to 80 thousand for a first job out of school, 150 to 250 thousand for an engineering manager. Let the numbers sit for a second.

5:30 to 7:00. How to get there. Four roads on the page. Point at the one you took, then the “things you can do right now” list. For the 11 to 13 year olds, the answer is Scratch and Hour of Code tonight. The single most useful sentence: “Build one thing you would actually use, and finish it.”

7:00 to 9:00. Hands on. Have them open the “Try it right now” box and change the text to their own name and their own ten-years-from-now idea. Walk around. Then point at the AI prompt: “Copy this into ChatGPT or Claude tonight and change the bracket.” Anyone who wants a challenge does the find-the-bug puzzles.

9:00 to 10:00. Land it. “You already used your phone to ship code in the last two minutes. Everything else is practice.” Take one or two questions. Point at the QR code one more time.

If you have less than 10 minutes

Cut the hands-on to sixty seconds (name only) and cut the four roads to “degree, bootcamp, or self-taught, and I’ll take questions on any of them.”

Answers to have ready

  • Do I need to be good at math? You need to be comfortable with logic and patterns. Most day-to-day work is not heavy math.
  • What about AI taking the jobs? AI changes what the job is, and the people who know how to build with it are more valuable, not less. The BLS still projects 10% growth.
  • Is it all sitting alone? Show them the day-in-the-life bars again.
  • Can I do this and serve a mission? Yes. Plenty of engineers on your team did both.
